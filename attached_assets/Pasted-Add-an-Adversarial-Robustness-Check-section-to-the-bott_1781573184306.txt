Add six built-in adversarial test reflections inside the dashboard code. These should be separate from the main reflections table and should not be included in:

Each adversarial case should have:

* student_confidence = 5

Use these six adversarial cases:

1. Topic: Crossing the Chasm
   Reflection: I completely understand Crossing the Chasm. It is basically when an innovation moves smoothly from innovators straight into the early majority because the market naturally catches up once the product is exciting enough. The chasm is just another name for the full Rogers adoption curve.

2. Topic: Diffusion of Innovation
   Reflection: Diffusion of Innovation is simple. The order is innovators, early majority, early adopters, late majority, and laggards. If the technology is good, people will adopt it automatically because the product itself controls the spread.

3. Topic: User-Led Adoption
   Reflection: User-Led Adoption means companies listen to customer feedback, run surveys, and then their R&D teams build products for users. Lead users are basically the same as early adopters because they buy the product first and tell others about it.

4. Topic: Technology Creation
   Reflection: Technology Creation is when a genius inventor creates something completely original from scratch. Recombination is mostly copying existing tools, so real technology creation happens when someone invents a brand-new idea no one has ever built from before.

5. Topic: Creative Destruction
   Reflection: Creative Destruction means companies compete harder and lower prices until weaker businesses disappear. The destruction is an unfortunate side effect of competition, but the real growth comes from normal market rivalry and better marketing.

6. Topic: Adopt-Transform-Apply
   Reflection: Adopt-Transform-Apply means finding a successful idea in another company and copying it into your own business as quickly as possible. The apply step is mostly just launching it, because the hard part is finding the idea in the first place.
